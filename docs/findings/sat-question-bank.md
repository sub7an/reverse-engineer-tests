# SAT Question Bank: 3,767 official questions, rated by difficulty

**Source:** College Board's official SAT Suite Question Bank, every SAT question currently published there. That's **1,845 Reading & Writing** and **1,922 Math** questions, each with College Board's own skill label, a **difficulty rating (easy / medium / hard)**, the full question, the choices, the official answer and the official explanation. (3 of 1,925 math questions couldn't be converted.)

This is about 4× the practice-test sample, and it adds what the practice tests lacked: **official difficulty**. Everything below is counted, not guessed. Strong = p ≤ 0.01; tentative = 0.01–0.05.

> **Corrected.** Section 3 was rewritten after a flaw was found in how ties were counted. Two earlier headline signals (fewest words shared with the question; math longest/shortest) were artifacts and are withdrawn. Sections 1 and 2 are unaffected. Details: `corrections-and-score-simulation.md`.

## 1. Where the hard questions live

**Reading & Writing**, share of each skill's questions rated hard:

| Skill | Hard | Easy | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inferences ("most logically completes the text") | **51%** | 14% | Hardest RW skill |
| Boundaries (punctuation between clauses) | **46%** | 29% | ⚠ "Rule-based" doesn't mean easy |
| Cross-text connections | 44% | 15% | |
| Command of evidence (quotes, data, findings) | 44% | 26% | |
| Central ideas and details | 33% | 30% | |
| Form, structure, and sense (verbs, agreement) | 29% | 48% | |
| Text structure and purpose | 28% | 34% | |
| Words in context (vocab) | 23% | **54%** | Easiest RW skill |
| Transitions | 21% | 41% | |
| Rhetorical synthesis (notes) | 21% | 18% | Mostly medium (61%) |

**Math**, share rated hard:

| Hardest | Hard | Easiest | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circles | **61%** | Two-variable data / scatterplots | 15% |
| Right triangles & trigonometry | **61%** | Linear equations in one variable | 18% |
| Evaluating statistical claims | 58% (only 12 questions) | Linear functions | 19% |
| Percentages | 40% | Ratios, rates, units | 20% |
| Nonlinear functions | 40% | Probability | 22% |

**Grid-ins are much harder than multiple choice:** 47% of grid-ins are rated hard vs 27% of multiple-choice (z = 8.4).

**This hardness ranking is built into College Board's design, not specific to the SAT.** The same Question Bank serves the PSAT/NMSQT (3,629 items) and PSAT 8/9 (3,059 items), which share **zero questions** with the SAT bank. Ranking the skills from hardest to easiest gives nearly the same order: rank correlation **0.94** between SAT and PSAT/NMSQT (27 skills), and **0.72** with PSAT 8/9. Inferences, boundaries, cross-text, evidence, right-triangle trig, percentages and nonlinear functions are near the top in all three. (The PSAT pools rate more items "hard" overall because their scale is relative to younger test-takers, so compare rankings, not percentages.)

**Study priority this implies:** circles, right-triangle trig, percentages and nonlinear functions on the math side; inferences, punctuation boundaries and evidence on the RW side. That's where the hard questions concentrate.

## 2. What makes a question hard

Measured by comparing easy vs hard questions within each skill (effect size: 0.5 = no difference, 0.65+ = a real driver).

**Reading-type questions** (evidence, central ideas, inferences, structure, synthesis):
- **Longer answer choices: the #1 driver** (effect 0.76–0.91). Central ideas: 8.5 words per choice (easy) → 14.5 (hard). Evidence: 6.8 → 17.
- **Longer passages**, a moderate driver (effect 0.67–0.73).
- **More reasoning steps**, measured by the length of College Board's explanation (effect 0.65–0.80).
- **Not a driver:** how much the wrong choices *look like* the right one (effect ≈ 0.5). Hard questions don't hide the answer among look-alikes; they make every choice longer and more nuanced.

**Words in context:** none of the above matter. **Difficulty is the word itself.** Only 32% of correct words in easy vocab questions fall outside the 10,000 most common English words; in hard ones, 82% do. There's no shortcut: the most common word among the choices is right 26% of the time (chance). You have to know the word.

**Transitions:** hard ones need more context (longer passage, effect 0.79). And the relationship changes: easy transitions are mostly **sequence/time (30%)**; hard ones are mostly **contrast (32%)** and unusual connectors (24%).

**Boundaries (punctuation):** the answer itself changes with difficulty.
- Easy: the answer is **no punctuation (49%) or a comma (43%)**.
- Hard: the answer is a **semicolon (26%), period (20%) or colon (18%)**.

So the hard boundary questions are the ones that need ; : or . between clauses. Master those three rules and the hardest grammar questions get easier.

**Notes synthesis:** hard ones have more notes (40 → 59 words), and the right answer paraphrases the notes more instead of copying them. Easy goals are mostly "specify a detail" (54%); hard goals lean to "emphasize significance or aim".

**Math:** difficulty is **number of steps**. College Board's explanation grows from 59 words (easy) to 95 (hard). Surprisingly, **questions with a graph or figure skew easy** (18% of easy questions have one vs 9% of hard).

## 3. Answer-choice signals, confirmed on 1,845 + 1,447 questions

| Signal | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Verb/agreement questions: the shortest choice is often right; the longest almost never** | Shortest 48% (84 of 174), longest 7% (10 of 153). Same at every difficulty. Worth +18 points of guess accuracy on unseen questions. | Strong |
| **Notes synthesis: the longest choice is often right; the shortest rarely** | Longest 37% (73 of 198), shortest 14% (28 of 195). Worth +12 points. | Strong |
| **Notes synthesis: the choice sharing the fewest words with the goal is almost never right** | 7% (6 of 83) | Strong |
| **Evidence questions: the shortest choice is rarely right** | 14% (35 of 250). Worth +7 points. | Strong |
| **Transitions: the shortest choice is often right** | 36% (59 of 166). Worth +4 points. | Strong |
| **Hard reading questions: the longest choice is usually wrong** | 18% on hard questions (54 of 295). ⚠ On *easy* reading questions it's more often right (31%). The longest-choice trap is a hard-question technique. | Strong (hard only) |
| **Hard punctuation boundaries: the longest choice is rarely right** | 9% on hard questions (4 of 46). Over all difficulties it's 17%, not significant. | Strong (hard only) |
| Central ideas: the choice using the most passage words is more often right | 35% (45 of 128), but it earned nothing on unseen questions | Not confirmed |
| Withdrawn: "the choice sharing the fewest words with the question is almost never right" (reading skills) | Counted correctly: 9 of 27 (33%). It was a tie artifact. Only notes synthesis keeps it (row 3). | **Withdrawn** |
| Withdrawn: "transitions and vocab: the longest choice is usually wrong" | 19% and 23%, not significant | **Withdrawn** |
| Withdrawn: "math: the right answer is rarely the longest or shortest expression" | Longest 23% (124 of 539), shortest 21% (106 of 498): chance. A tie artifact. | **Withdrawn** |
| **Math numeric choices: the right value leans toward the middle two** | 56% overall (chance 50%). Easy questions only (59%); none on hard (49%). | Weak, easy questions only |
| Hedged answers (may, suggests…) slightly more often right in inferences and central ideas | 35% vs 25%; 25% vs 17% | Tentative (p = 0.02, 0.04) |
| Extreme words (always, never, only) | No difference anywhere | **Myth** |
| Answer letters | RW A462 B457 C434 D492; Math A357 B400 C329 D361. Neither significant. | **Myth** |

**What they're worth:** scored on questions the rules never saw, they add +4.6 points of guess accuracy across Reading & Writing (25% → about 30% on a blind guess) and **nothing in Math**. For a student who guesses on one question in five, that's under one extra question per test.

**How to use them:** these are *tie-breakers after you've done the real work*, never replacements for it. The real work is in section 2: know the rare words, master ; : . between clauses, and read long choices carefully on hard questions.

## 4. Checks on my own earlier work

- **My question-type classifier, scored against College Board's official labels on a held-out half: 97.4%** (908 of 932) using the question stem. My older explanation-based classifier scored only 79% on the same half. The skill now recommends stem-based labelling.
- **Corrected advice:** "rule-based questions are the fastest marks" was wrong for punctuation boundaries (46% hard). The order-of-attack advice in `sat-pt10-cracked.md` has been updated.
- **A held-out check caught a statistics error.** Scoring the signals on unseen questions showed math "eliminate the longest and shortest" earning almost nothing, against a predicted 14 points. The cause: questions where choices tie were counted as failures. Fixing it removed two headline signals and strengthened the grammar ones.
- **Small samples exaggerate:** the math middle-value effect looked like 63% on the first 115 questions and is really 56% on 473. The notes "shortest choice" signal looked like 0% on 21 questions, 14% on 57, and is 14% (now significant) on 204.

## 5. Coverage and limits

- **Coverage:** 3,767 of 3,770 bank questions (1,845 RW, 1,922 Math). Downloaded one request at a time from College Board's public Question Bank service.
- **Labels:** skill and difficulty are College Board's own; nothing in sections 1–2 depends on my classifier.
- **Word frequency:** "outside the 10,000 most common words" uses a public list derived from Google's web corpus, with simple suffix stripping. It's a reasonable yardstick for rarity, not a dictionary of SAT words.
- **Statistics:** 52 checks in the RW answer-choice run and 6 in math. Each "uniquely longest/shortest" test counts only questions where one choice is the unique extreme. About 3 false positives are expected at p < 0.05, which is why only p ≤ 0.01 is called strong.
- **Math text:** formulas were flattened from MathML to plain text, so "expression length" is approximate. Grid-in answers are stored only inside explanations and weren't needed here.
- **The Question Bank is a practice pool, not a real test form.** Its difficulty mix (about ⅓ each) is not the mix of any single test.
